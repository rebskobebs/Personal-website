Urban Political Realignment and
Electoral Shift in Kathmandu

A study of urban political discontent in Kathmandu — how it reshapes voter behavior and drives the shift from mainstream parties to alternative political movements, drawing on 22 semi-structured interviews with voters, political leaders, and civil society members.

Work in Progress

The academic paper is still being written. A working draft is available below. Findings from this research were published in The Annapurna Express.

View Working Draft (PDF)

Overview

This research investigates urban political discontent in Kathmandu and how growing dissatisfaction with mainstream parties is reshaping voter behavior and driving support for alternative political movements. Nepal's post-2006 democratic settlement generated high expectations that established parties have struggled to meet — producing cycles of hope, frustration, and political realignment.

The study draws on 22 semi-structured interviews conducted with voters, political party leaders, and civil society members across Kathmandu during fieldwork in 2025. The interviews explored lived experiences of political disillusionment, perceptions of party performance, and the appeal of emerging alternative movements.

Research Questions

  • What drives urban voters in Kathmandu to abandon established parties?
  • How do alternative political movements appeal to discontented urban constituencies?
  • What does this electoral shift reveal about the structural conditions of Nepal's democracy?

Key Themes

  • Affective anti-incumbency: A generalized hostility toward whoever holds power, regardless of party — driven by perceived corruption, elite capture, and unmet promises.
  • Youth disillusionment: The particular alienation of younger Kathmandu voters who evaluate politics against democratic expectations rather than a pre-2006 baseline.
  • Alternative movements: The rise of parties like Rastriya Swatantra Party and what their appeal reveals about the limits of anti-establishment politics within existing institutions.
  • Urban–rural divergence: How Kathmandu's political trajectories diverge from rural Nepal, reshaping the national electoral landscape.

Methodology

The research uses qualitative methods — primarily semi-structured interviews — analyzed through thematic coding in Atlas.ti. Interview subjects include voters across age groups and occupations, representatives of mainstream and alternative political parties, and civil society leaders active in Kathmandu's political space. Electoral data is used to contextualize and corroborate qualitative findings.
